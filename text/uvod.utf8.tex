The increasing power of computers and the growth of their use by a wide mass of users in the global computer network
demands greater attention to the security of these users.
For this network includes
not only people and organizations with good intentions, but also those who attempt
to violate the right to privacy by intercepting confidential
information of others and using it to their advantage or to compromise that person in other ways, for example
by impersonating them. Therefore, it is important to make this activity practically impossible for these malefactors by converting confidential information
into a form that can only be read by selected persons and cannot be read by any undesired person
-- confidentiality.
Furthermore, verification of the identity of the originator of transmitted information must be part of this conversion -- authentication.
Algorithms that ensure confidentiality and authentication of transmitted data are studied by cryptography.
These algorithms are also called ciphers. With ever-increasing use of ciphers and the discovery of newer
types of cryptanalysis, their lifespan is decreasing.
Lessons from the history of ciphers show that a cipher was unbreakable until
someone discovered its weakness. Therefore, a new cipher always had to be created immediately afterward.
Nowadays, continuous research and development of new ciphers is ongoing, which should be more resistant
to ever-newer types of attacks.

This work deals with a component of encryption algorithms called the substitution box
(abbreviated as S-box) and its development. Both purely
mathematical approaches using the theory of Galois fields and various stochastic design methods are used for its construction,
including evolution, which is employed in this work.
Evolutionary computation techniques are, as the name suggests, inspired by evolutionary theory from biology.
The aim of the work is to use certain properties of substitution boxes
as objective functions for their evolutionary development.
Although many properties have already been studied, relatively little work has been carried out
in searching for S-boxes, particularly in multi-criteria genetic search.

However, it was first necessary to design and implement a collection of algorithms
that would make it possible to experimentally determine these properties.
This implementation must primarily enable rapid specification of various evolutionary experiments and, if possible,
execute them as quickly as possible.

This work was created as an independent work that does not build on any school project.
